A test build of Cross-Com Enhanced, free community servers for *Tom Clancy's Ghost Recon: Future Soldier*'s online play. This one lets you **host a server for your friends from the launcher, in one click**, brings a server for Windows, and fixes port forwarding on many routers. It's still for public testing: expect rough edges, and expect accounts and stats to be reset before a later build. **Your reports are what this build is for.**

If you have 0.1.2, the launcher offers this update in the bar under its title.

## What's new

**Host a server from the launcher**
- **A new Host screen** in the side menu runs a Cross-Com server on your PC, built into the launcher, while the launcher is open. Your group gets its own accounts, stats, leaderboards and news.
- **Choose who can join**: *My network and VPN* (friends in your house, or on Tailscale, ZeroTier or Radmin VPN with you, with nothing opened on your router), or *Anyone on the internet with a join code*.
- **Start server** lets it through Windows Firewall (Windows asks once). For the internet, it also asks your router to forward the server's ports (UPnP, PCP or NAT-PMP), and the community test network then **checks your server from outside**, so you know your friends will reach it. *Reaching it* shows each step, and what to do when one fails.
- **Join codes**: one for each way friends reach you (the internet, your network, your VPN), with a **Copy** button. Friends paste the code into their launcher's server box. A code is the server's address, its port and its own HTTPS certificate's fingerprint, so there's no domain or certificate to buy, and their launcher talks to your server and no other, encrypted.
- **Any port works.** Many routers won't forward port 80, which the game always asks for; the join code carries the port your router gave, and the client sends the game there.
- **Play on my server** sets your own game up to play on it, with your name.
- **Stop server**, or closing the launcher, stops it and takes its router forwards away.

**A server for Windows**
- **`crosscom-server-windows-x86_64.exe`**, below: the same server as on Linux, one file, for a Windows PC that hosts on its own, without the launcher (at startup too, with Task Scheduler). With `https_port = 80` it makes its own certificate, and players join with a join code.

**Port forwarding fixed on many routers**
- The client asks the router in the terms its UPnP service uses: routers offering only the newer `WANIPConnection:2`, or `WANPPPConnection` (routers that dial their own internet connection), refused the old requests.
- A router listing more than one connection is asked on the one it uses, and one that only keeps forwards with no time limit gets one.
- When the router refuses, the launcher and the logs now say the router's own reason, instead of *"The gateway does not have any free ports"* for every refusal.
- The game's port is forwarded sooner after you go into Multiplayer, so your connection's check is right from the start.

**Your game profile, without the name prompt**
- **The game no longer asks for a profile name** the first time you play with an account: Cross-Com gives the account a profile before the game looks, a copy of the one you last played on this PC (your options and campaign come along, from Ubisoft's days too) or a new one, named after the account.
- **Settings › Saves** lists the game's profiles on this PC, backs them all up into a folder you choose (with Cross-Com's own saves), restores a backup, and switches the profile your account plays. Whatever it replaces is kept first.

**And**
- **The wiki**: [a help desk](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced/wiki) for every launcher screen and setting, your connection, hosting a server (from the launcher, on Windows, on Linux), port forwarding and routers, troubleshooting and error messages.
- The game's logs now name your Windows edition rightly (*Windows 10 Pro*, not *Enterprise*).

## Play

1. Download **`crosscom-launcher.exe`** below. It's the only file you need: the client and the server are built in. Keep it in a folder of your own (your Desktop or Documents, not Program Files), so it can update itself.
2. Run it. The setup finds the game, lets it through Windows Firewall (one Windows permission prompt), makes your account on the community test network, `grfs-test.jdevwebb.net` (you choose a name: there's no password), and installs the client into the game, keeping the game's own files. To play on a friend's server, choose **Another server** and paste their join code.
3. Press **Play**.

You need your own copy of the game, and of any DLC pack you want to play: nothing of the game is included, and we can't help you get it. More in the [README](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced#play) and the [wiki](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced/wiki/Getting-Started).

## Run your own server

From the launcher's **Host** screen ([step by step](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced/wiki/Hosting-from-the-Launcher)), or on its own: `crosscom-server-windows-x86_64.exe` on Windows ([step by step](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced/wiki/Windows-Dedicated-Server)), and on Linux the program or Docker, with HTTPS through Caddy or of its own: see [Hosting](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced/blob/main/docs/hosting.md). Its image for this release is `ghcr.io/jdevwebb/crosscom-server:0.1.3`, signed with the release (`IMAGE`, `IMAGE.sig`). Upgrade the server with the launcher: join codes on another port than 80 need the new client, and the check from outside needs the server.

## Good to know

- **Hosting for the internet needs a public address.** If your provider shares one address between many customers (a *carrier-grade NAT*, common on mobile, satellite and some fibre connections), or your router can't forward ports, host for your network and a VPN instead. The Host screen says which it is. [Port Forwarding and Routers](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced/wiki/Port-Forwarding-and-Routers) explains.
- **A server hosted from the launcher has open sign-up**: anyone who can reach it can make an account. A join code isn't a password, so share it with the people you play with, and stop the server when you're not playing. A dedicated server can ask for a sign-up code.
- **While you host, your own game's connection may show Moderate**: the server checks your game through your own router. Your friends can still reach your matches.
- **Voice chat hasn't been tested yet.** It needs real microphones.
- **Some players can't reach each other directly** when both their routers are strict, or behind their provider's shared address. The overlay's Server tab and **Settings › Network** tell you how reachable you are. A relay for those players is planned for 0.2.0.
- **Microsoft Defender may remove the launcher as soon as it's downloaded** (`Trojan:Win32/Bearfoos.B!ml`, a machine-learning guess). It's a false positive: the files aren't code-signed yet. Restore it from **Windows Security › Protection history**, check it against the verification details below before you run it, and exclude its folder and the game's: step by step in [Antivirus warnings](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced/wiki/Antivirus-Warnings). The server the launcher hosts (in `%APPDATA%\Cross-Com\Server`) may be flagged too.
- **Found a problem?** Open an issue with what happened and roughly when. A security problem goes through the Security tab instead: see [SECURITY.md](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced/blob/main/SECURITY.md).
