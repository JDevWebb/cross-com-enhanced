The first test build of Cross-Com Enhanced: free community servers for *Tom Clancy's Ghost Recon: Future Soldier*'s online play, and a launcher that sets the game up for you. Every adversarial mode, co-op and Guerrilla, friends, squads and challenges are online again. It's released for public testing: expect rough edges, and expect accounts and stats to be reset before a later build. **Your reports are what this build is for.** (It replaces 0.1.0, which was withdrawn: see below.)

## What's fixed since 0.1.0

0.1.0 was taken down soon after it was published: **the game stopped at start with "Entry Point Not Found"** (`?Close@SavegameWriter@orbitclient@mg@@QAEX_N@Z could not be located`). The release build links the client with Microsoft's linker, which cut the names of the client's Orbit functions short; the builds we tested used another linker, which kept them whole. The client now exports them in a way both linkers keep, and every build, CI's and the release's, now fails unless the client exports each function by the exact name the game asks for.

Nothing else changed from 0.1.0: this is the build 0.1.0 should have been.

## Play

1. Download **`crosscom-launcher.exe`** below. It's the only file you need: the client is built in. Keep it in a folder of your own (your Desktop or Documents, not Program Files), so it can update itself.
2. Run it. Setup finds the game, lets it through Windows Firewall (one Windows permission prompt), makes your account on the community test network, `grfs-test.jdevwebb.net` (you choose a name: there's no password), and installs the client into the game, keeping the game's own files.
3. Press **Play**.

You need your own copy of the game, and of any DLC pack you want to play: nothing of the game is included, and we can't help you get it. More in the [README](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced#play).

## What works

**No passwords**
- Your launcher makes an identity the first time it runs, a key it keeps on your PC (encrypted for your Windows user). Your account belongs to it: the launcher signs you in with it, and the game gets a sign-in token of its own, good for this game on this server only. Nothing to remember, and nothing a leaked game setting could reuse elsewhere.
- Playing on another PC: copy your identity in **Settings › Server and account** and use it there. See [Your identity](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced#your-identity).

**Matches**
- Every adversarial mode, ranked and player matches: Conflict, Decoy, Saboteur and Siege, and the DLC modes Stockade (Arctic Strike) and Takeover (Khyber Strike), on every map. A Quick Match starts at the game's own 8 players (6 in the smaller modes); its host can lower that in the overlay's Lobby tab.
- The campaign and Guerrilla in co-op, with friends invited from the lobby.
- Players host their matches as the game always did; the server introduces them, through home routers too, and players behind one router reach each other across their own network. If the host leaves, the match moves to another player.
- The client asks your router to forward the game's port while you play (UPnP, PCP or NAT-PMP), and checks the forward lets players in, so others reach you whatever their own router does.
- The launcher updates itself: a newer release is offered in a bar under its title, and installed (with the client to match) when you press Update, once the release key's signature and the file's checksum check out.
- The server checks it can reach each host's game from outside. Players who can't punch through aren't sent to hosts they couldn't reach, and the overlay's Server tab tells you if others can't reach your game, and what to change. The launcher's Play screen keeps what your last game found, beside the firewall and port forwarding.

**Friends and progression**
- Friends lists, requests, invitations into a lobby, the party following its host, and lobby chat.
- XP, levels, unlocks, Gunsmith loadouts and stats, kept on the server, and a friend's stats from View Player Stats.
- Squads: make one, invite, accept, ranks, the motto and the message of the day.
- Multiplayer Challenges: the Daily Friend Challenge and Beat Your Best, and challenging a friend back.
- The server's news in the Ghost Feed and on Community Messages.

**DLC packs**
- Quick Match only finds you lobbies your game can load: the client tells the server which packs you have.
- The launcher lists the packs it finds and warns you what a missing one keeps you from playing.

**The in-game overlay** (<kbd>F5</kbd>)
- The server's status and how many players are online, its news, and leaderboards for every player on the server, by mode (the game's own Leaderboards screen can't be opened on PC).
- The Lobby tab: how many players your Quick Match or squad room needs before its countdown starts, and the most players the lobbies you make take (2 to 12).

**The launcher**
- A guided setup: the game's folder and its DLC packs, the server, your account (a name, signed for by your identity, over HTTPS), and the game's options (skipping the intro videos, DirectX 9 for older graphics cards).
- Restoring the game's own files, in Settings.
- A connection test, in Settings: whether other players can reach your game (open, moderate, strict or blocked), and what to change if they can't. See [Your connection](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced#your-connection).
- Sending each online game's log to the server, so problems can be found without you having to describe them. You can turn it off in Settings; what's sent, and when, is in the [README](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced#whats-sent-and-when).

Tested end to end on real copies of the game, on Windows 11 and under Wine on Linux, with up to four players, on one network and between home networks over the internet.

## Run your own server

The server is one program, for Linux, in Docker with HTTPS through Caddy, or on its own: see [Hosting](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced/blob/main/docs/hosting.md). Its image for this release is `ghcr.io/jdevwebb/crosscom-server:0.1.1`, signed with the release (`IMAGE`, `IMAGE.sig`). Players' accounts belong to their identities; accounts you make yourself (`add-account`, for test players and bots) have a password instead. A server on the internet needs HTTPS (a domain, and Caddy, which the compose files set up): the launcher and the client only talk to it encrypted; a server on your own network or VPN can do without. `crosscom-server connections` shows how players' joins went: how many ended early, by each player's router, so you can tell who has trouble connecting ([Connection records](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced/blob/main/docs/hosting.md#connection-records)).

## Good to know

- **Voice chat hasn't been tested yet.** It needs real microphones; it's on this build's test list.
- **Port forwarding is new in this build** and hasn't met many routers yet. If joining or hosting fails, run the connection test and put its details in your report.
- **The game runs fullscreen only.** It has no windowed setting, and writes its fullscreen setting back each time it starts.
- **A lobby's size is fixed when it's made.** The overlay's *Most players* applies to the lobbies you make next.
- **Microsoft Defender removes the launcher as soon as it's downloaded** (`Trojan:Win32/Bearfoos.B!ml`, a machine-learning guess). It's a false positive: the files aren't code-signed yet. Restore it from **Windows Security › Protection history**, check it against the verification details below before you run it, and exclude its folder and the game's: step by step in [Antivirus warnings](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced#antivirus-warnings).
- **Found a problem?** Open an issue with what happened and roughly when. A security problem goes through the Security tab instead: see [SECURITY.md](https://github.com/JDevWebb/cross-com-enhanced/blob/main/SECURITY.md).
